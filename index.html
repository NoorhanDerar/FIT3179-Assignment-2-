What separates highly rated restaurants in Malaysia from the rest?

Is a 4.5-star restaurant really better than a 4.1?

Reviews of Langkawi restaurants average 4.2 stars on Google and 4.6 on TripAdvisor. Same island, almost four-tenths of a star apart. So what does a star rating actually tell you, and who is it measuring?

A restaurant's rating depends on where it is, which platform you check, and who is reviewing. A 4.5 doesn't mean the same thing everywhere. Scroll down to see why, and what to check before you trust a score.

361,784 Google and TripAdvisor reviews from ten cities

1 · Who gets reviewed

Reviews come from a few places, not all of Malaysia

Behind every star rating are people who chose to write a review, and they are not spread evenly. We looked at 361,784 reviews from ten cities. On Google, Petaling Jaya alone has 48,628 reviews, while Shah Alam has only 1,869.

The ten cities also sit in just 8 of Malaysia's 16 states and federal territories. Those eight are home to about 70% of Malaysians, but Sabah, Kelantan, Pahang and Terengganu are not in the data at all.

Whatever the stars tell us, they mostly tell us about a handful of cities.

2 · Do stars separate good from bad

Almost everyone gives four or five stars

About 8 in 10 reviews on both platforms are 4 or 5 stars, and about half are a perfect 5. In every 100 reviews, roughly 10 on Google and 7 to 8 on TripAdvisor are 1 or 2 stars. So the scale is squashed at the top, and small differences in the average can hide a lot.

The same pattern shows up in every city. Each row below is one city, and a darker square means a bigger share of that city's reviews gave that many stars.

When almost everything scores high, the number you look at matters less than what sits behind it.

3 · Does the platform matter

Same city, different stars

Google and TripAdvisor rate the same cities differently. Petaling Jaya scores 4.13 on Google and 4.62 on TripAdvisor, Langkawi 4.21 against 4.59, and Penang 4.22 against 4.52. Kuala Lumpur, Ipoh and Johor Bahru barely differ, and Melaka and Miri go the other way (4.09 against 3.97, and 4.12 against 4.02).

Take care with the biggest gaps: Petaling Jaya's TripAdvisor score comes from 1,304 reviews of only 6 restaurants, Langkawi's from 31 restaurants and Penang's from 52.

A simple "tourists rate higher" story doesn't fit everywhere: Kuala Lumpur and Melaka are well visited too, yet TripAdvisor gives them no bonus. One possible explanation is that the two platforms attract different reviewers. TripAdvisor reviews are about three and a half times longer, which suggests people use the two sites differently.

The score depends partly on where you look, so one platform isn't the full picture.

4 · Inside the number

Two restaurants with the same average can be very different

On Google, Langkawi, Kuala Lumpur and George Town all average about 4.2, but 10.8%, 9.5% and 8.3% of their reviews are one or two stars. Langkawi's restaurants are also the most spread out: the middle half of them covers almost twice as many rating points as Kuala Lumpur's. The TripAdvisor boxes for Langkawi and Petaling Jaya are less reliable, because TripAdvisor lists only 31 and 6 restaurants there.

Across all cities, about one in five TripAdvisor restaurants averages 4.5 or higher, against about one in ten on Google. So a 4.5 is rarer on one platform than the other.

An average hides the spread, so look at how many bad reviews are behind a good score.

5 · Does the food type matter

Cuisine is only a weak hint

Does a Chinese, Indian or Malay restaurant rate higher than the rest? Since the data has no cuisine info, we guessed each restaurant's cuisine from its name. That works for about 4 in 10 restaurants, so the chart shows only cuisines with at least 20 restaurants on a platform.

The differences are small. On Google, Western restaurants sit 0.17 stars above average and cafés and bars 0.10 above, while Chinese and seafood restaurants sit about 0.16 below. On TripAdvisor the gaps are similar: Western is 0.09 above and Chinese is 0.25 below. Even the biggest gap is a quarter of a star, so a good cuisine label will not rescue a poor restaurant.

Treat cuisine as a hint, not a ranking: it covers fewer than half of the restaurants.

6 · What people say

Happy and unhappy reviews talk about different things

Happy reviews are full of general praise: "great", "delicious", "friendly", "best". Unhappy reviews tell a story of what went wrong. On TripAdvisor they say "ordered", "asked", "told" and "after". On Google they say "service", "slow", "poor" and "expensive". These words go along with high or low ratings, which is not proof of what caused them.

Praise is general, but complaints are specific: ordering, waiting, service and price.

7 · Does money explain it

Richer states don't clearly rate higher

Income differs sharply across Malaysia. The median household in Selangor, Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya earns over RM10,000 a month, against RM4,083 in Kelantan.

So do richer states have better-rated restaurants? We compared each city's state income with its share of 5-star reviews. The Google trend line is flat, and the TripAdvisor one rises only slightly. The highest scores, in Petaling Jaya, Langkawi and George Town, rest on just 6, 31 and 52 TripAdvisor restaurants. With only ten cities, treat this as a hint.

Money doesn't explain the stars much. The platform and who is writing seem to matter more.

8 · Over time

The same star score means less each year

On TripAdvisor, the average star score in reviews rose every year, from about 4.1 in 2015 to about 4.5 in 2020. So a 4.5 that looked outstanding in 2015 would have been just average by 2020. Which cities lead has been steadier. Langkawi and Penang are in the top three every year from 2015 to 2020, and Melaka is ranked 7th or lower every year. Petaling Jaya tops the list in 2018 and 2020, but on only 135 and 187 reviews in those years.

A star rating is a snapshot, so check when the reviews were written as well as how many there are.

9 · What to do with this

How to read a star rating

  1. Check how many people reviewed it. A 4.8 from a handful of reviewers means much less than a 4.2 from thousands.
  2. Check the share of 1–2 star reviews. Two places with the same average can have very different numbers of bad experiences.
  3. Compare two platforms. If a place is great on one and ordinary on the other, ask why.
  4. Read the complaints, not just the praise. Unhappy reviews name what went wrong, such as slow service or price.
  5. Remember the date. Scores creep up over time, so a 4.5 today may be nothing special.

Limits

We can't know who wrote a review or why. The ten cities lean towards busy and tourist areas, and they cover only 8 of Malaysia's 16 states and federal territories. Only restaurants listed on these two platforms are included, and the reviews were collected in 2022. Cuisine is guessed from restaurant names, and about 6 in 10 names don't reveal it. Some city and platform pairs rest on very few restaurants, such as TripAdvisor in Petaling Jaya (6), Langkawi (31) and Penang (52). Only TripAdvisor reviews have dates. These are patterns in this data, not proof of cause.
