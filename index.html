What separates highly rated restaurants in Malaysia from the rest?

Is a 4.5-star restaurant really better than a 4.1?

Reviews of Langkawi restaurants average 4.2 stars on Google and 4.6 on TripAdvisor. Same island, almost four-tenths of a star apart. So what does a star rating actually tell you, and who is it measuring?

A restaurant's rating depends on where it is, which platform you check, and who is reviewing. A 4.5 doesn't mean the same thing everywhere.

1 · Who gets reviewed

Reviews come from a few places, not all of Malaysia

Behind every star rating are people who chose to write a review, and they are not spread evenly. We looked at 361,784 reviews from ten cities in just 8 of Malaysia's 16 states and federal territories. Income differs sharply too: the median household in Selangor, Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya earns over RM10,000 a month, against RM4,083 in Kelantan.

Whatever the stars tell us, they mostly tell us about a handful of cities.

2 · Do stars separate good from bad

Almost everyone gives four or five stars

About 8 in 10 reviews on both platforms are 4 or 5 stars, and about half are a perfect 5. In every 100 reviews, roughly 10 on Google and 7 to 8 on TripAdvisor are 1 or 2 stars. So the scale is squashed at the top, and small differences in the average can hide a lot.

When almost everything scores high, the number you look at matters less than what sits behind it.

3 · The surprise

Same city, different stars

Google and TripAdvisor rate the same cities differently. Petaling Jaya scores 4.13 on Google and 4.62 on TripAdvisor, Langkawi 4.21 against 4.59, and Penang 4.22 against 4.52. Kuala Lumpur, Ipoh and Johor Bahru barely differ, and Melaka and Miri go the other way (4.09 against 3.97, and 4.12 against 4.02).

Visitors alone are unlikely to explain this, because Kuala Lumpur and Melaka are busy with visitors too, yet show no TripAdvisor bonus. One possible explanation is that the two platforms attract different reviewers: TripAdvisor reviews are about three and a half times longer (a median of 267 characters against 75).

Take care with Petaling Jaya: only 1,304 TripAdvisor reviews, from 6 restaurants.

The score depends partly on where you look, so one platform isn't the full picture.

4 · Inside the number

Two restaurants with the same average can be very different

On Google, Langkawi, Kuala Lumpur and George Town all average about 4.2, but 10.8%, 9.5% and 8.3% of their reviews are one or two stars. Langkawi's restaurants are also the most spread out: the middle half of them covers about twice as many rating points as Kuala Lumpur's. The tiny TripAdvisor boxes for Langkawi and Petaling Jaya just reflect how few restaurants it lists there.

Across all cities, about one in five TripAdvisor restaurants averages 4.5 or higher, against about one in ten on Google.

An average hides the spread, so look at how many bad reviews are behind a good score.

5 · What people say

Happy and unhappy reviews talk about different things

Happy reviews are full of praise words: "best", "great", "delicious", "friendly". Unhappy reviews tell a story of what went wrong. On TripAdvisor they say "ordered", "asked", "told" and "after". On Google they say "service", "slow", "poor" and "expensive". These words go with high or low ratings, not proof of what caused them.

Praise is about the food. Complaints are about what happened between ordering and eating.

6 · Does money explain it

Richer states don't rate higher

We compared each city's state income with its share of 5-star reviews. The Google trend line is flat, and the TripAdvisor one rises only slightly. The highest scores, in Petaling Jaya, Langkawi and George Town, rest on just 6, 31 and 52 TripAdvisor restaurants. With only ten cities, treat this as a hint.

Money doesn't explain the stars. The platform, and who is writing, matter more.
